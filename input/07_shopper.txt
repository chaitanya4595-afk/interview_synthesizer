Interview ID: INT07
Role: Shopper (retiree, long-time customer)
SYNTHETIC: fictional interview for demonstration

Interviewer: Thank you for agreeing to talk with us today. Could you start by telling me how you usually shop?

Interviewee: Oh, well, I've been shopping at Northfield, gosh, it must be 40 years now. Back when it was the old building, before they put in the new one with the big windows. My late husband used to say it was the only store in town where the butcher knew your name. That was Walter, the butcher, he retired, I think he moved to Florida. Or was it Arizona. His daughter married one of the Hendricks boys. Anyway.

Interviewer: How often do you shop there now?

Interviewee: Twice a week, usually Tuesday and Friday. Tuesday is senior discount day, which isn't much but it adds up. Friday I go because my grandson comes on the weekend and I like to have things for him. He's very picky. He'll only eat the chicken nuggets shaped like dinosaurs. I don't understand it, a nugget is a nugget, but you know how children are. His mother lets him get away with everything.

Interviewer: Do you buy Northfield's store brand products?

Interviewee: Some. The canned tomatoes, I buy those. They're just as good as the other kind, I've been using them in my sauce for years and nobody's ever said anything. The sugar, the flour. I bake a lot, or I used to. My hands aren't what they were. The arthritis, you know. I went to a new doctor for it and he told me to eat less bread, which, I said, you're asking a lot.

Interviewer: Are there store brand products you don't buy?

Interviewee: The coffee. I tried it once. No. I buy my coffee, the one in the red can. It used to be cheap, now I think it's $6.49. Can you believe that? I remember when coffee was nothing. Everything is so expensive now. The gas, the electric bill. My electric bill last month was more than my car payment used to be.

Interviewer: If the store brand coffee were better, would you switch?

Interviewee: Maybe. I'm set in my ways. But if it was good, sure. I'm not paying six dollars just for the can. Although I do like the can. I keep buttons in the old ones. I have a whole shelf of them in the garage. My daughter says I should throw them out but you never know when you'll need a can.

Interviewer: What do you think of how the store brand looks on the shelf?

Interviewee: It's a bit plain, isn't it? The labels. They look like something from a hospital. I don't mind it, I'm not buying it to look at it. But my daughter, she won't buy it, she says it looks cheap. She shops at that fancy store across town with the cheese counter. Spends a fortune. I tell her, you're paying for the name. She doesn't listen.

Interviewer: What would you think if Northfield carried more of its own brand and fewer name brands?

Interviewee: Well, I'd want to keep my coffee. And my tea, I like a particular tea. Other than that I don't care much. If it's cheaper I'm all for it, I'm on a fixed income. But don't take away the things people like. People get upset. When they moved the pharmacy counter to the other side of the store, you'd have thought it was the end of the world. Mrs. Kowalski wrote a letter to the newspaper. The parking lot is also a problem, by the way, there's a pothole near the cart return that's been there for months. Somebody's going to twist an ankle. I told the young man at the service desk. He said he'd pass it on.

Interviewer: Thank you, I'll note that. Going back to the store brand, is there anything that would make you more likely to try new store brand items?

Interviewee: If someone gave me a taste. They used to have the ladies with the little samples on Saturdays. I liked that. You'd try the sausage or the cheese and sometimes you'd buy it. They stopped doing that. I think it was the pandemic. A lot of things stopped with the pandemic. My church group stopped meeting in person and never really started again the same way.

And the coupons. I still clip coupons from the paper. Well, there aren't many in the paper anymore. It's all on the phone now. I don't do the phone thing. My grandson tried to show me the app and I just didn't take to it. If they had paper coupons for the store brand, I'd try more of it.

Interviewer: Is there anything else you'd like Northfield to know?

Interviewee: That they have good people there. The girl at the checkout, the one with the braids, she always helps me with my bags. And fix that pothole. And bring back the samples. And keep my coffee, please.

Interviewer: Thank you so much for your time.

Interviewee: Oh, you're welcome, dear. Is this going to be on the news?
