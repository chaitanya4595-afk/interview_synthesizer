Interview ID: INT06
Role: Shopper (parent of three, budget-conscious)
SYNTHETIC: fictional interview for demonstration

Interviewer: Thanks for talking with us. Can you tell me a bit about how you shop for groceries?

Interviewee: Sure. I've got three kids, the oldest is a teenager, so I'm basically feeding a small army. I do one big shop a week at Northfield, usually Sunday, and then I stop at the discounter maybe every other week for bulk stuff. I spend about $210 a week at Northfield, sometimes more if there's a birthday or something.

Interviewer: Why Northfield for the main shop?

Interviewee: It's close, it's clean, and they have everything. The discounter is cheaper but they don't carry the brands my kids want, and the produce isn't as good. Also I can get everything in one trip at Northfield. With three kids, one trip matters.

Interviewer: How often do you buy Northfield's store brand?

Interviewee: A lot, actually. More than I used to. Probably a third of my cart now. Milk, eggs, bread, butter, cheese, canned stuff, pasta, frozen veggies, paper towels. For basics I don't see the point in paying extra. When prices went up a couple of years ago, I started switching one thing at a time and most of it was fine.

Interviewer: Where don't you buy the store brand?

Interviewee: Cereal, for sure. My kids will not eat the store-brand cereal. I tried. They know. Snacks too, granola bars, chips, the stuff that goes in lunchboxes. I think part of it is the kids care what their friends see. And coffee. I tried the store-brand coffee once and went right back to my regular. It was just not good.

And diapers, back when I was buying them. I tried the store brand and it leaked. That was the last time I bought store-brand anything for the baby. Once you've had that happen you don't take the chance.

Interviewer: What would make you try the store brand in those categories?

Interviewee: If it was a lot cheaper. Like, not a few cents. If a box of cereal is five dollars and the store brand is three, okay, I'd try it again for myself, maybe not for the kids. Or if someone I trust said it was good. My sister is the one who got me on store-brand frozen vegetables. She said they were the same thing and she was right.

Also, sampling would help. I'm not going to spend money on something to find out it's bad. If they gave out samples or had a money-back guarantee, I'd be more willing.

Interviewer: What would you think if Northfield replaced a lot of brand-name products with its own brand?

Interviewee: Depends which ones. If they took away my coffee or my kids' cereal, I'd be annoyed. I might buy those at the discounter or order them online. Once I'm going somewhere else for a few things, I might start going there for more things. So that could backfire.

If they just made the store brand better and cheaper and left the brands there, I'd probably buy more of it. I like choice. I don't want to be forced.

Interviewer: How do you feel about the packaging and look of the store brand?

Interviewee: It's fine, I guess. It looks like a store brand. Kind of plain. The discounter's store brand actually looks nicer, which is funny. Their stuff looks like it could be a real brand. Northfield's looks cheap next to it. I don't care that much for things like foil, but for stuff I put on the table, it does matter a little.

Interviewer: Have you noticed any issues with the store brand being out of stock?

Interviewee: Sometimes. The store-brand milk was out a few times this summer. When that happens I just grab the regular brand, it's not a big deal for milk. But if they had fewer brands to choose from, and the store brand was out, that would be a problem.

Interviewer: If you could tell Northfield one thing about their store brand, what would it be?

Interviewee: Make it cheaper for real, keep the brands for the stuff kids want, and fix the coffee. [laughs] Honestly, I'd buy more store brand if I trusted it more. It's not that I love brands. I just don't want to waste money on something my family won't eat.

Interviewer: Thank you, this is really helpful.

Interviewee: No problem.
