# Themes

## Theme 1: The store brand's cheap image, dated packaging and a few bad products (coffee above all) hold back growth, and one bad product costs trust across the line.
Support: 8 interviews [INT01, INT02, INT03, INT04, INT05, INT06, INT07, INT08] | Mixed: 0 | Against: 0
Key quotes:
* "You can't double share with a brand people are embarrassed to put in their cart." [INT08]
* "You lose somebody on one product and they don't try the next one." [INT01]
* "Honestly, quality is usually a spec problem, not a manufacturing problem." [INT04]
Why it matters for the case question: Adding items to a brand shoppers distrust grows shelf space, not share, so doubling needs a brand and spec fix alongside new items.

## Theme 2: Supplier capacity and lead times limit how fast private label can grow: dairy and frozen are near capacity, new items take most of a year, and store-brand in-stocks are already weak.
Support: 6 interviews [INT01, INT02, INT03, INT05, INT06, INT08] | Mixed: 1 [INT04] | Against: 0
Key quotes:
* "Our plant is running at close to 90% most of the year, and in summer we're basically at capacity because of ice cream." [INT05]
* "For a new product on an existing line, figure 9 to 12 months from agreed spec to product on the shelf." [INT04]
* "Our lines are running at about 70% utilization right now, so there's room." [INT04] (Against; INT04 is Mixed because it also says "I'd be careful about assuming all suppliers have spare room like we do.")
Why it matters for the case question: Growth can move faster where suppliers have room, which one dry grocery co-packer says it has, but dairy and frozen need new capital or new suppliers first, and every new item needs deciding soon.

## Theme 3: Doubling chain-wide in two years is a stretch, and one chain number does not fit stores that start from very different levels.
Support: 3 interviews [INT01, INT03, INT08] | Mixed: 0 | Against: 1 [INT02]
Key quotes:
* "I'll be honest, I think that's a stretch in two years." [INT08]
* "doubling in two years feels like a headquarters number, not a store number." [INT03]
* "We're at about 27% of sales in private label, and I think we could be higher if we had more of it." [INT02] (Against)
Why it matters for the case question: The two-year doubling target is contested, with the suburban and rural managers and the VP calling it too fast while the value-store manager sees room, so the target should flex by store and category.

## Theme 4: Shoppers will move to store brand in basics and where it is cheaper and good, but not in brand-loyal categories, and pulling loved brands sends them to competitors.
Support: 6 interviews [INT01, INT02, INT03, INT06, INT07, INT08] | Mixed: 0 | Against: 0
Key quotes:
* "the people who left were mostly the price shoppers, and they went there for the private label, basically." [INT03]
* "If I take their ketchup off the shelf, I'm giving them a reason to leave too." [INT03]
* "If they just made the store brand better and cheaper and left the brands there, I'd probably buy more of it." [INT06]
Why it matters for the case question: Growth should come from adding and improving store brand beside the brands shoppers love, not from delisting them.

## Theme 5: The margin prize is smaller than the headline gap suggests once a visible price gap, better specs, packaging costs and lost trade funding are netted out.
Support: 4 interviews [INT03, INT04, INT05, INT08] | Mixed: 1 [INT02] | Against: 0
Key quotes:
* "Our private label items carry a gross margin somewhere around 32 to 35%, compared to about 25% on the comparable national brands." [INT08]
* "If we cut their space significantly, some of that goes away." [INT08]
* "Private label items run roughly 8 points more gross margin than the brand equivalent in my store, sometimes more." [INT02] (Against; INT02 is Mixed because it also says "The price gap has to be real, like 20 to 30% under the brand, not five cents.")
Why it matters for the case question: The profit case for doubling depends on how much of the gross margin gap survives reinvestment, which no interviewee has quantified.

## Theme 6: Stores and the headquarters team lack the capacity to execute resets and new items at the pace doubling requires.
Support: 5 interviews [INT01, INT02, INT03, INT04, INT08] | Mixed: 0 | Against: 0
Key quotes:
* "The last full reset we did on center store was something like 40 labor hours per aisle once you count pulling product, moving shelves, new tags, and fixing the planogram mistakes." [INT01]
* "What I'd say is do it category by category, not all at once." [INT02]
* "Their private label team isn't that big." [INT04]
Why it matters for the case question: A phased, category-by-category rollout with funded labor is more realistic than doubling everywhere in two years.

## Conditions for success
* Product quality matches the brand, with specs not cut to hit cost: raised by [INT01, INT02, INT04, INT05, INT08]. "One, the product has to be as good as the brand, not almost as good." [INT01]. Numbers: none
* Packaging refreshed to look modern: raised by [INT01, INT04, INT06, INT07, INT08]. "And if Northfield wants a packaging refresh, which I'd recommend, their current look is dated, that's a one-time cost too." [INT04]. Numbers: none
* A price gap big enough for shoppers to feel: raised by [INT02, INT03, INT06, INT08]. "The price gap has to be big enough that customers feel it." [INT08]. Numbers: 20 to 30% under the brand [INT02]
* Store brand reliably in stock: raised by [INT01, INT02, INT03, INT04, INT06]. "The worst thing that can happen is I train a customer to buy our brand of rice and then it's not there on Saturday." [INT02]. Numbers: 6% out-of-stock on store-brand milk [INT01]
* Stores get labor hours for resets: raised by [INT01, INT02, INT08]. "Stores need labor to execute the resets." [INT08]. Numbers: 40 labor hours per aisle [INT01]
* Northfield commits multi-year volumes so suppliers commit capacity: raised by [INT04, INT05, INT08]. "So before we add people or equipment, we'd want a multi-year agreement with minimum volumes." [INT04]. Numbers: none
* Targets flex by store cluster and category rather than one chain number: raised by [INT01, INT03, INT05, INT08]. "Just that one number for the whole chain doesn't fit every store." [INT01]. Numbers: none
* Roll out category by category, starting where shoppers are ready: raised by [INT02, INT08]. "We should have started with something like frozen vegetables or household paper, where people don't care as much." [INT08]. Numbers: none
* Keep the brands shoppers love on the shelf: raised by [INT01, INT03, INT06, INT07]. "But don't take away the things people like." [INT07]. Numbers: none
* Test first in ready stores: raised by [INT02, INT03]. "And you have to let the stores that are ready go first." [INT02]. Numbers: none
* Faster supplier payment terms: raised by [INT04, INT05]. "We're on 60-day terms right now, which is tough for a business like ours with high fixed costs." [INT05]. Numbers: 60-day terms [INT04, INT05]
* New items decided soon, given lead times: raised by [INT04]. "For a new product on an existing line, figure 9 to 12 months from agreed spec to product on the shelf." [INT04]. Numbers: 9 to 12 months
* New capital or new suppliers for dairy and frozen: raised by [INT05]. "Roughly $4 million in capital, and well over a year to get it built and running." [INT05]. Numbers: $4 million
* Trial support such as samples, guarantees or coupons: raised by [INT06, INT07]. "If they gave out samples or had a money-back guarantee, I'd be more willing." [INT06]. Numbers: none
* Share store sell-through data with suppliers: raised by [INT04]. "If we could see what's actually selling, we'd plan better and they'd have fewer out-of-stocks." [INT04]. Numbers: none
* Trade funding losses netted against the margin gain: raised by [INT08]. "If we cut their space significantly, some of that goes away." [INT08]. Numbers: none

## Where interviewees disagree
* Pace of doubling (Theme 3): [INT02] says "We're at about 27% of sales in private label, and I think we could be higher if we had more of it.", while [INT03] says "doubling in two years feels like a headquarters number, not a store number." and [INT08] says "I'll be honest, I think that's a stretch in two years."
* What drives shoppers away: [INT02] says shoppers leave when store brand is missing ("When I don't have it, they go to the discounter up the street."), while [INT01] ("They ask for the product at the service desk, and if we don't have it they go to the other grocer down the road."), [INT03] and [INT06] say they leave when brands are missing.
* Where to start: [INT08] says "We should have started with something like frozen vegetables or household paper, where people don't care as much.", while [INT05] says "Our plant is running at close to 90% most of the year, and in summer we're basically at capacity because of ice cream."
* Size of the margin prize, partly a difference of scope (Theme 5): [INT02] says of one store "Private label items run roughly 8 points more gross margin than the brand equivalent in my store, sometimes more.", while [INT05] says of dairy "It's real, but it's smaller in dairy than people think."
