# Critic review: Northfield private label memo (rewrite, 399 words)

**Summary**
1. The Answer mostly follows from the evidence: flex the target by store cluster and category, and don't double chain-wide in two years. But Title 3 ("Lead with dry grocery") contradicts it. Dry grocery passes the supply test, but it holds the weakest-demand categories (coffee, cereal, snacks, and the failed snack test). INT03's "fresh first" dissent is still not mentioned. Open question 3 also says we don't yet know which categories pass.
2. Overstatements: "All eight interviews agree" (Title 2), "before adding shelf space" (Title 2), "will beat" (Title 1), "expects" (Title 4) and "fresh supply is tight" (Title 3, with no fresh evidence in the body). Ignored disagreements: INT02 backs doubling, INT03 wants fresh first, and the size of the margin gain is disputed (INT04, INT05).
3. Every quote is verbatim, and every number is attributed to one interview. Nearly all conditions raised by 2 or more interviews are in the Answer. The exceptions are that "net of lost trade funds" drops INT04's higher spec and packaging costs, and a blanket [INT01-INT08] citation makes the condition list impossible to audit. The 11 number FAILs in quality_report.md are false positives from the checker (see Other checks).

**Verdict count (31 claims):** Supported 14, Thin 8, Overstated 8, Unsupported 1. There are also 3 ignored disagreements and 1 partial missing condition.

**Word budget:** The memo is at 399 of 400 words, so every fix has to free up words. Cuts: "All eight interviews agree." (4 words, overstated anyway). Open question 3 (about 9 words; it contradicts Title 3 and repeats the Answer). Also replace the blanket condition list with three grouped buckets (see #4).

## Other checks

- **Does the Answer follow from the evidence?** Mostly. "Flex by cluster and category, not chain-wide in two years" follows. "Start where demand, supply and margin align" follows. The body then breaks it: Title 3 leads with a category chosen on supply alone (#1), and open question 3 says the qualifying categories are unknown (#14). The Answer also never gives a direct no, and never says what chain-level share the staged plan is expected to reach.
- **Ignored or mischaracterized disagreements:** (a) INT02 supports doubling, and only INT04 is shown dissenting (#9). (b) INT03 wants fresh first while the memo leads with dry grocery. This was raised in the last review and is still open (#1). (c) The size of the margin gain (INT04, INT05 vs INT08) is left out (#11). (d) Dairy quality (INT02 "as good as the brand" vs INT05 and INT01) is covered up by "All eight interviews agree" (#6). The INT04 dissent itself is quoted fairly.
- **Action titles a partner would push back on:** Title 1 "will beat" is an unproven prediction, and "staged" is undefined (#8). Title 4 gives two instructions and no insight (#10). Title 5 dodges the shelf-space trade-off and mixes two ideas (#16). Title 3 contradicts the Answer (#1).
- **Numbers as general fact:** None. Every number (400, 70%, 90%, 32 to 35%, 25%, 20 to 30%) is attributed to its interview. The risk is in the titles, which turn one plant's figure into a category fact (#3).
- **Quotes:** All 9 memo quotes match the transcripts verbatim. None are misattributed.
- **quality_report.md:** It covers this memo version (the line text matches). Quote pass is 9/9. All 11 number FAILs are false positives. The checker reads digits from bare interview ids written inline ("INT08 calls", "INT04 reports", "beyond INT05") as numbers, while ids inside brackets are skipped. The true memo number failure count is 0, so the 66.7% rate is misleading. Fix the checker to ignore `INT\d{2}` tokens, then rerun it. The checker cannot catch any of the semantic issues above.
